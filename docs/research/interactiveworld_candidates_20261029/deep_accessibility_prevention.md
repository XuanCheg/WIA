## NARRATE-12802 — incremental clearing for a bounded placement

**Source.** [Complete TaskPack](/local_home2/zhouxinyang/models/InteractiveWorld/benchmark/tasks/taskpacks/accessibility/NARRATE-12802.taskpack.json). Relevant pointers: `/story/premise`, `/story/assessment_contract/required_evidence`, `/story/evidence`, `/executable_spec/world/objects`, `/executable_spec/goal/goals`, `/story/interaction/completion_contract`, `/runtime/oracle_commands`.

**Actual task and evidence.** Three similar bottles occupy the area between a pot and bowl. The assistant observes successive openings after moving bottles to an adjacent holding counter and decides when the scale can be placed. The evidence ledger states that one and two removals remain insufficient and the third exposes the full gap. The oracle removes left, middle and right bottles, looks at the clearing counter after each, then retrieves and places the scale at a specified spot. This is changing geometric evidence, not external semantic knowledge or a learned dynamics rule.

**What is scored.** Executable goals require the scale on the clearing counter, all three bottles on the holding counter, and both anchors unmoved. Native process checks require three successful counter looks, each bottle transfer and transfer-before-look subsequences. They forbid grabbing either anchor and forbid acquiring the scale before each of the three transfers. This meaningfully scores staged process; it also fixes the required number of removals. The release rubric adds the separate transition criterion that the scale stays at source until all bottles clear and anchors never depart baseline. Neither native final goals nor this criterion alone specifies a numerical gap margin, full-footprint containment between anchors, or gripper approach clearance. `on(scale, clearing_counter)` is weaker than those geometric claims.

**Human removal and WIA judgment.** A robot can inspect, move bottles and place the scale directly. The original human communication burden disappears, leaving familiar GF clearance and SR separation. T22–T25 already require physical feasibility with support/release; D1-1–D1-3 require continuous dimensional passage. The incremental feedback is a useful extension, but this source begins in a known cluttered state and does not diagnose an unexpected execution deviation. Provisional verdict: **conditional**, primarily GF, not evidence that AP-4 is filled.

**Robot formulation and counterfactual.** Goal: fit and stably release a carried item within a bounded usable region while preserving anchors. Knowledge: observed target footprint and evolving free space. Decision: which blocker to move, whether the latest free region admits the placement and approach, and when to stop. Outcome: legal grasp/release, full supported footprint, anchor preservation, and collision-free approach/withdrawal. A paired condition should change the target footprint or one blocker position so that two versus three removals are sufficient, while controlling model, clutter count, grasping, budget and destination. Correct actions must change; the fixed all-three source contract cannot be carried over unchanged. Difficulty comes from clearance estimation and repeated verification, not bottle count alone. Native geometry and robot reach remain unverified; no new physics is needed for a rigid-body version.

## NARRATE-14004 — undo a wrong-object pickup before resuming

**Source.** [Complete TaskPack](/local_home2/zhouxinyang/models/InteractiveWorld/benchmark/tasks/taskpacks/accessibility/NARRATE-14004.taskpack.json). Pointers: `/executable_spec/world/objects`, `/story/prologue/actions`, `/story/evidence`, `/story/interaction/completion_contract`, `/runtime/human_policy/initial_state`, `/runtime/oracle_commands`.

**Actual task and evidence.** A trigger glass-cleaner bottle, bath-salt jar and small baby-oil bottle have different catalogued models and no added labels. The prologue inspects the cabinet and cleaner, then grabs the cleaner; at handoff the wearer holds it while asking to take the supposed baby oil to a side table. The oracle returns cleaner, looks at baby oil, selects it, transfers it and verifies the side table. Evidence supports a wrong identity and a reversible physical state. The semantic distinction concerns product identity/intended use; the source grounding contract says `common_sense_allowed: false`, so chemical danger should not be presented as an evaluated external-knowledge inference.

**What is scored.** Final goals require baby oil on the side table, cleanser on its cabinet and bath salt unmoved. The native contract requires return-cleanser → acquire-baby-oil → walk-to-side-table as a subsequence, forbids acquiring oil before returning cleanser, forbids cleanser delivery and any bath-salt acquisition. The release terminal criteria add stable safe cleanser placement, while its separate transition gate checks return before acquisition and salt preservation. The oracle includes looking at baby oil, but the completion contract does not itself require that look. Neither toxicity nor application to skin is simulated by these goals.

**Human removal and WIA judgment.** Replace the mistaken human pickup with a robot-owned wrong held object, observed through its mounted sensors. The robot then diagnoses mismatch, safely stages the wrong object, and resumes the unchanged goal. GG already covers target identity selection; T01–T03 cover supplied order and allow retries. The incremental value is diagnosis and recovery from an already changed state, not a new object category. Provisional verdict: **priority as an AP-4 seed**, conditional on independent recovery choices and valid physical trajectories; the existing source is a scripted human mix-up, not a demonstrated autonomous failure recovery benchmark.

**Robot formulation and counterfactual.** Goal: deliver the requested product while restoring the mistakenly held item and preserving distractors. Knowledge: appearance-grounded identity, intended product and observed held state. Decision: continue or undo, choose a safe support, verify return, and reacquire the correct item. Outcome: stable correct delivery, wrong item safely supported, empty withdrawn hand, protected distractors. Pair otherwise identical states with correct versus incorrect item in the gripper: one branch proceeds, the other returns and switches. Balance model/side mapping so trigger shape or a fixed cabinet side is not the answer by itself. Difficulty is object verification plus constrained staging; a free second gripper could remove the staging need and must be controlled. Product chemistry is unsupported and unnecessary for this placement-based recovery probe.

## NARRATE-81009 — stage a payload, clear a hazard, and resume placement

**Source.** [Complete TaskPack](/local_home2/zhouxinyang/models/InteractiveWorld/benchmark/tasks/taskpacks/accessibility/NARRATE-81009.taskpack.json). **Actual task and evidence.** A wearer carries a new blue cup toward a table with two protected cups and broken glass across its front edge. A clear adjacent table supplies temporary storage and a broom supplies the intended cleanup action. The oracle sets the new cup down, acquires the broom, wipes the glass area, returns the broom, reacquires the cup and places it between the two existing cups. This sequence is a useful resource constraint: the occupied hand makes temporary staging relevant.

**What is scored.** Executable final goals include only the new cup on the hazard table and both existing cups unmoved. Broken glass and broom are explicitly unscored objects. The native completion contract nevertheless requires the staging, broom acquisition, Wipe, broom return and cup reacquisition sequence; direct glass/cup acquisition is forbidden. It forbids Wipe before cup staging and final placement before broom return. Thus cleanup is not absent from process scoring. However, the visual-state event makes broken glass invisible after broom return, with `require_offscreen: true`. This establishes an authored visual disappearance, not measured fragment transport into a container. The release rubric separately demands contained glass and broom restoration, and a transition gate says hazard resolution precedes broom return and cup placement. A source Wipe command or visibility event cannot automatically establish that stronger release claim.

**Human removal and WIA judgment.** Direct robot action retains staging, tool use and resumption. These can extend AP-4 beyond the existing prescribed-order stack tasks and GG occlusion removal, provided an actual recoverable obstruction changes action feasibility. A fixed initially present hazard is still precondition handling; stronger AP-4 requires an unexpected but reversible deviation during execution. Provisional verdict: **conditional**, with a material physical implementation gap.

**Robot formulation and counterfactual.** Goal: place the payload after making the approach region usable, leaving protected objects untouched and cleanup tool restored. Knowledge: observed obstruction, occupied gripper and free staging support. Decision: stage, choose tool or alternative legal removal, verify cleared region and resume. Outcome: actual obstacle displacement/containment, clear swept volume, stable final cup and tool placement. Counterfactual: move the obstruction out of the needed approach region while preserving appearance, tool, payload and supports; direct placement becomes correct without staging/cleanup. For a recovery pair, introduce a physically reversible blocker after initial inspection and verify that revision is necessary. Difficulty includes long tool reach, tabletop contacts and regrasping. Glass fragmentation, sharpness, injury and broom collection are unsupported here. A rigid removable blocker could test the recovery mechanism, but would be an explicit redesign rather than evidence that glass cleanup already works.
